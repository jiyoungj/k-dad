Database Description

1. Data collection

This section describes the recording procedures, experimental settings, and equipments used during the data collection process.

1.1. Equipments used

Two types of data were collected simultaneously: articulatory and acoustic.

Articulatory data: a three-dimensional electromagnetic articulograph (Carstens AG501)
Acoustic data: a TASCAM US-4x4 audio interface & a SHURE KSM44A microphone

1.2. Experiment setup

All recordings were conducted in a sound-attenuated booth at the Hanyang Institute for Phonetics and Cognitive Sciences of Language, Hanyang University, Seoul, South Korea.

Participants were seated comfortably while wearing the sensor coils. A total of eight receiver coils were attached to the following articulators: the tongue tip, tongue body, tongue dorsum, upper lip, lower lip, lower incisor, upper incisor, and the nose bridge (nasion). The upper incisor and nose bridge sensors served as reference points for correcting head movement during recording.

To establish a consistent spatial coordinate system, a flat plastic bite plane was used prior to recording. This procedure defined the occlusal plane as the horizontal (x-axis) and the perpendicular direction as the vertical (y-axis), resulting in a rotated Cartesian coordinate system for all articulatory measurements. A standard calibration procedure was performed at the beginning of each recording session.

1.3. Recording procedure

Pre-experiment training
Prior to the main recording session, all participants completed an approximately 30-minute training session. This session familiarized them with the experimental setup and allowed them to practice the speech materials, ensuring stable and natural production of the intended prosodic patterns.

Data recording
Recordings were conducted in the sound-attenuated booth, where articulatory and acoustic data were collected simultaneously. Articulatory movements were captured using the Carstens AG501 system, while the acoustic signal was recorded via a TASCAM audio interface and a SHURE KSM44A microphone. (For details on data post-processing and sampling rates, see Section 5.)

Stimulus presentation
Participants were presented with two types of speech materials displayed on a computer screen placed 60cm away from the participant: (i) Korean paragraphs and (ii) monosyllabic Korean words.

Repetition and randomization
Each paragraph was produced twice, with a different randomization order applied to each repetition. If a participant hesitated or failed to produce the intended prosodic pattern, the trial was repeated to ensure data quality and consistency.

5. Technical specifications

This section explains how recordings and annotation files are organized and named in the database, data processing procedures, sampling rate, and related technical details.

5.1. Labeling conventions

All files follow the naming convention: [TaskType]_[Label]_[Sentence]_[Rate]_[Dialect][Age][Sex][ID]_[Repetition]
The fields are defined as follows:

  • TaskType: PA (Text Passage) or SY (Isolated Syllables)
  • Label: the passage identifier (A–G) for passage recordings, or the syllable set identifier for syllable recordings
  • Sentence: the specific sentence within a passage, or the specific syllable within a set
  • Rate: F (Fast), N (Normal), or S (Slow)
  • Dialect: dialect region code, e.g. SEK (Seoul), NKK (North Gyeongsang), SJK (South Jeonnam), etc.
  • Age: speaker's age group (20–60)
  • Sex: M (Male) or F (Female)
  • ID: speaker number within the group (01–30)
  • Repetition: R01 (first repetition) or R02 (second repetition)

Each recording consists of three files sharing the same base filename:

  • wav audio file,
  • mat articulatory data file (MATLAB format), and
  • TextGrid text annotation file.

TextGrids contain three interval tiers:

  1. 발화 — sentence-level segmentation in Hangul orthography;
  2. Utterance — the same sentences in Google Korean Romanization; and
  3. 단어 / Word — word-level segmentation with each label giving the Hangul form and its romanization separated by a forward slash (e.g., 북풍과/bugpunggwa).

5.2. Data processing details

Recordings were made as continuous sweeps of complete text passages and complete syllable sets. Each sweep was subsequently segmented into individual files at the sentence level for passage recordings and at the individual syllable level for syllable recordings. Following segmentation, TextGrid annotation files were created for each recording through hand-labeling in Praat, providing sentence-level transcriptions in both Hangul and Google Korean Romanization, as well as word-level segmentation.

Raw sensor position data were processed in MATLAB using a script developed by Mark Tiede (Haskins Laboratories). Head movement correction was performed using the nasion and upper incisor/gum reference sensors to remove the effects of head movement during recording. Following head correction, sensor coordinates were rotated into the occlusal plane via bite plane correction, which was recorded from each speaker prior to the main recording session. The .mat files distributed with the database contain the fully head-corrected and bite-plane-corrected sensor trajectories.

Acoustic and articulatory signals were recorded simultaneously on a single system.

5.3. Sampling rate

Acoustic recordings are sampled at 48,000 Hz at 16-bit depth and stored in WAV format.
Articulatory data are sampled at 1,250 Hz and stored in MATLAB (.mat) format.
